## 3. CLINICAL COMMENTARY — TOP FEATURES

### morph_V2_saddleback_score_max
Direction of effect : higher → NOT Type 1 Brugada (Type 2/3 or Normal variant)
Clinical confidence : MEDIUM
Clinical threshold  : Complementary to covedness score
Sensitivity/Spec    : Useful for Type 1 vs Type 2 discrimination

Physiology:
Saddleback morphology (rising ST after J-point, secondary hump, upright T-wave) is characteristic of Type 2 Brugada or normal variants mimicking Brugada. High saddleback score in V1–V2 is evidence AGAINST Type 1.

------------------------------------------------------------
### st_V3_st_area_above_baseline_n_valid
Direction of effect : larger → Brugada
Clinical confidence : MEDIUM-HIGH
Clinical threshold  : Proportional to severity
Sensitivity/Spec    : Robust amplitude-integrated measure

Physiology:
The area under the ST curve above baseline integrates both the height and duration of ST elevation. It captures the total ST "burden" rather than a single timepoint, making it more robust to J-point localization error.

------------------------------------------------------------
### st_V2_st_convexity_max
Direction of effect : more positive (convex) → Brugada Type 1
Clinical confidence : MEDIUM (100Hz limits precision)
Clinical threshold  : Sign change is clinically meaningful
Sensitivity/Spec    : Discriminative for coved vs saddleback

Physiology:
The second derivative of the ST segment reflects its curvature. A positive value indicates the segment curves upward (convex = coved pattern). A negative value indicates concavity (saddleback). This is the mathematical encoding of what cardiologists visually assess as the ST "shape".

------------------------------------------------------------
### st_V3_st_j40_j0_ratio_mean
Direction of effect : higher → Brugada (Type 1) or saddleback (Type 2)
Clinical confidence : HIGH
Clinical threshold  : Absolute elevation > 0.1 mV significant
Sensitivity/Spec    : Primary Type 1 screening measure

Physiology:
ST amplitude at J+40ms reflects the sustained elevation after the J-point. In coved-type Brugada, this remains elevated but begins descending; in saddleback, it may be rising toward the secondary hump.
